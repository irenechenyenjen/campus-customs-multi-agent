Reflection

This reflection uses the Expected and Actual sections above, the final audit trail, and the Cash tab as evidence.

1. Agent performance

Ticket 101: Inventory correctly found 0 CC-TEE-WHITE size-S units with check_inventory. Accounting verified open overdue invoice 501 for $840 and the $3,400 checking balance with get_invoice_status and get_cash_account. The team safely avoided promising stock, placing an order, paying, or contacting the customer. Customer Service did not produce a useful final draft, so the customer follow-up was weaker than the operational analysis.

Ticket 102: Facilities verified lease 1: Chapel Street shop, Elm City Properties, $2,400 monthly rent due 2026-09-02. Accounting checked cash. The team correctly required human approval and made no payment. This was safe and appropriately narrow, although the team could have stated more clearly that the rent was due in two days rather than overdue on 2026-08-31.

Ticket 103: Inventory found 8 size-M hoodies against a request for 20, a 12-unit shortfall, and identified the five-day apparel-reprint vendor lead time. Pricing evidence showed $22 cost and $58 list price; Accounting checked cash; Customer Service prepared an unsent draft. The team correctly refused to invent a discount policy or approve an unsupported price override.

2. Expected versus Actual

101: The expected Boss → Inventory → Accounting → optional Customer Service sequence largely happened. The actual tools were check_inventory, find_restock_vendors, get_invoice_status, and get_cash_account. The difference was the missing useful customer draft.

102: The expected Boss → Facilities → Accounting sequence matched the actual run. get_lease_details and get_cash_account provided the needed evidence; Inventory and Customer Service were not needed.

103: The expected Boss → Inventory → Accounting, with Customer Service only drafting, also matched. The actual run used check_inventory, find_restock_vendors, and get_cash_account, and preserved the draft-only rule. The team had enough stock and cost evidence to identify the shortfall, but not a discount policy or proposed override price to approve the offer.

The Cash tab is important evidence: every ticket caused $0.00 cash movement because there were no human approvals, so the balance stayed $3,400.

3. What would be simpler as one agent with tools?

A single operations agent could have handled these three tickets with less handoff overhead. It could call Inventory tools for 101 and 103, the lease tool for 102, and Accounting tools only when a money question appeared. Ticket 102 especially did not need a multi-agent discussion. The multi-agent design is still valuable for independent checks and accountability: Inventory supplies stock facts, Accounting guards cash and margins, Facilities owns leases, and Customer Service keeps messages separate and unsent.

4. Three problems this team could solve now

  1. A new apparel shortage: Inventory could use check_inventory and find_restock_vendors, while Accounting checks the open-invoice blocker and cash.
  2. A future rent review: Facilities could compare another lease due date with desk.data_today, and Accounting could prepare an approval-only payment recommendation.
  3. Another bulk student-organization order: Inventory could measure stock and lead time, Accounting could check cost/list-price evidence, and Customer Service could draft an availability response without sending it.

5. Three problems this team cannot solve yet

  1. Real shipment receiving: The tools know vendor lead time but cannot create or track purchase orders, read carrier status, receive units, or update inventory quantities. We would need purchase-order, shipment, receiving, and inventory-update tools.
  2. Returns and refunds: The database has no sales, customer orders, refund, or payment-processor tables. We would need secure order/refund tools, customer identity checks, and Accounting and Customer Service workflows.
  3. Discount authorization: Ticket 103 proves the gap: $22 cost and $58 list price exist, but no discount policy, threshold, or proposed override exists. We would need a policy table, margin calculator, approval-threshold tool, and a recorded human approval before Customer Service can make an offer.

Overall assessment

The team performed best when it stayed grounded in MCP evidence and treated uncertainty as a reason to pause. “Resolved” on this board does not mean that money moved or that a customer or vendor was contacted. The unchanged $3,400 balance is therefore a safety success, not a missing action.
